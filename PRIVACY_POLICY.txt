JARVIS PRIVACY POLICY

Effective status: JARVIS is currently in private-use/pre-commercial
development and is not currently offered commercially. A geographic
business/contact address and any additional information legally required for a
commercial offering will be added to the operative Privacy Policy before
commercial launch.

1. Controller

The controller for current JARVIS private-use processing is Jamie Kanbier,
The Netherlands. Contact: jamiekanbier@gmail.com. A geographic address is
intentionally not published in the public repository during private-use/pre-
commercial development.

2. Current private-use/pre-commercial status

JARVIS is currently used for private development and is not currently offered
for sale or under a paid subscription. This policy describes the current
software and local development state, not a commercial service that does not
yet exist.

3. Scope

This policy covers first-party JARVIS processing in the current installation,
including its application services, local data stores, optional hardware
paths, and user-selected external capabilities. Third-party services have
their own policies.

4. Local-first architecture

JARVIS is designed for local-first operation. Data normally remains in the
local installation unless the user selects an external provider, API, website,
MCP server, device, or integration, or a later first-party service is clearly
provided and separately documented.

5. Categories of data

Depending on enabled features, JARVIS may process prompts and responses,
files, task and plan state, artifacts, settings, workspace/profile metadata,
memory and knowledge indexes, capability/package metadata, diagnostics,
permissions, audit records, model/provider metadata, and optional microphone,
camera, screen, or device observations. The application also processes
credential metadata and protected credential values through the secure
credential boundary.

6. User Model and memory

The User Model stores selected facts and preferences with provenance,
confidence, sensitivity, retention, and workspace scope. Episodic or
conversation memory is bounded and selective; JARVIS does not need to retain
every utterance. External text does not automatically become a personal fact.

7. Knowledge libraries

User-selected documents may be indexed into workspace-scoped Knowledge
Libraries. Source files remain under the user’s control; deleting an index
does not delete the source. Document content is treated as untrusted data,
including any instructions embedded in it.

8. Credentials and authentication

JARVIS uses trusted credential-management mechanisms and stores credential
metadata separately from raw secrets. Raw secrets are not intended for
ordinary model or tool context, logs, events, memory, artifacts, or backups.
Credential use is scoped; generated packages do not receive unrestricted
secret access. Machine-bound credentials may require reauthentication.

9. Generated capabilities and integrations

JARVIS may process generated integrations, workflows, procedures, package
metadata, and UI configurations. Generated code is subject to review,
sandboxing, certification, and brokered authority. It does not receive
unrestricted access to user data or credentials.

10. Logs and diagnostics

JARVIS retains bounded, sanitized audit, trace, health, and diagnostic records
where needed for security, verification, recovery, and operation. Sensitive
values are redacted or represented by references where applicable.

The current JARVIS version does not include first-party telemetry or analytics
collection sent to the Licensor. Local resource measurements used for runtime
decisions are operational data in the installation, not a promise that future
versions or user-selected third-party services will never collect telemetry.

11. Camera and microphone

Camera and microphone features are optional and separately configured. Captured
frames, audio, and transcriptions are bounded by the enabled runtime path and
are not treated as permanent retention by default. Microphone/listening mode
does not change permission or authority policy.

12. Third-party services selected by the user

User-enabled AI providers, APIs, websites, MCP servers, devices, and other
external services may receive the data necessary for the selected operation,
subject to the user’s configuration, classification policy, and the relevant
third-party terms and privacy policy. JARVIS does not control those services.

13. Purposes

Processing is used to provide local application functionality, execute and
verify user-directed tasks, maintain selected memory and knowledge indexes,
securely operate credentials, diagnose failures, recover state, prevent abuse,
and improve reliability through verified local evidence.

14. Legal bases where applicable

For a future commercial offering, applicable legal bases will be identified in
the operative commercial Privacy Policy for the relevant processing. In the
current private-use development state, processing is primarily initiated and
controlled by the user or developer in the local installation. Nothing here
removes a legal obligation that applies to a particular processing activity.

15. Local storage

JARVIS stores selected durable state in application-owned local stores and
external user-owned locations as documented by the relevant feature. Raw
credentials remain within the CredentialVault boundary. Application removal
does not by itself promise deletion of all durable local user state.

16. External transmission

Local JARVIS processing is distinct from user-selected external processing.
Data may leave the device when the user configures an external provider,
service, MCP server, integration, or other network capability, subject to
normal classification and permission controls. No claim is made that data can
never leave the device.

17. Retention

Retention is bounded by feature policy, workspace settings, artifact and
memory policies, audit/recovery requirements, and applicable law. User-managed
source documents are not deleted merely because an index or projection is
removed.

18. User control and deletion

Users may inspect, correct, delete, export, or change retention for supported
memory and user-state domains, subject to security, integrity, legal, and
recovery constraints. Credential deletion and revocation use the secure
credential service. Deleting a derived index does not delete its source.

19. Security

JARVIS separates trusted application authority from untrusted model,
external, and generated content. PermissionBroker, policy, approval,
CredentialVault, sandbox, data classification, audit, and recovery controls
remain separate from commercial entitlement and presentation channels.

20. Data-subject rights

For a future commercial offering, the operative Privacy Policy will describe
the applicable rights and how to exercise them, including access, correction,
deletion, restriction, objection, portability, and complaint rights where
applicable. Current local private-use controls are provided by the relevant
application services and local data stores.

21. Automated decision-making

JARVIS may route models, rank solutions, detect opportunities, or propose
actions. Model output and derived recommendations do not create trusted
approval or authority. High-impact actions remain subject to trusted policy,
permission, verification, and user controls.

22. Children

JARVIS is not currently offered as a child-directed commercial service. Future
commercial materials will address applicable age and child-data requirements.

23. Legal and regulatory requests

JARVIS may process or disclose information when required by applicable law or
valid legal process, subject to the available legal protections.

24. Business transfers

If a future lawful business transfer affects personal data, it will be handled
under applicable law and the operative Privacy Policy and notices then in
force.

25. Policy changes

This policy may be updated as JARVIS changes. Material future commercial
processing will require updated operative notices where applicable.

26. Supervisory authority

The operative commercial Privacy Policy will identify the relevant supervisory
authority and complaint route for the applicable user and processing. No
particular authority is inferred here for an as-yet non-commercial product.

28. Final local-first principle

JARVIS keeps processing local where practical and exposes external processing
as a separate user-selected choice. Local-first is a design principle, not a
promise that external processing can never occur.
